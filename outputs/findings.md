# Part 3: does baseline immune composition predict response to miraclib?

## Answer

No population differs significantly between the two arms.
The comparison is bounded rather than merely inconclusive: at 95% simultaneous confidence over all 5 populations tested, it rules out any shift larger than 1.32 percentage points, the widest interval being `monocyte`'s.
`monocyte` carries the largest estimated shift, +0.43 percentage points.

That is non-responder, 325 samples from 325 subjects; responder, 331 samples from 331 subjects.
Each subject contributes exactly one sample, so the observations are independent and the test's assumption holds.

### Why baseline, and not every timepoint pooled

Subjects in this trial are sampled repeatedly, so pooling timepoints would count several correlated samples from one subject as several independent observations.
That inflates the apparent sample size without adding a single subject, and a rank test fed pseudo-replicates returns p-values that are too small.
Restricting to baseline costs little: the arms still hold 325 and 331 subjects.

Baseline also answers the question that was asked.
A difference measured before treatment could be used to choose treatment; a difference that appears afterwards may be a consequence of responding rather than a predictor of it.

## Method

Mann-Whitney U, two-sided, on the relative frequencies from the Part 2 summary table.
Benjamini-Hochberg across the 5 populations that could be tested, at alpha = 0.05.
Shift is the Hodges-Lehmann estimate, the median of every pairwise difference, in percentage points and oriented so that a positive value means the non-responder arm is higher.
Its interval inverts the same test at 95% for that population alone and is not adjusted for multiplicity, so significance is judged by the q-value and magnitude by the interval.
Every interval in the results table is marginal at that level; the headline bound instead uses the Bonferroni-widened family, each interval taken at alpha / 5 so that all 5 hold together at 95%.
Effect size is P(x > y) + 0.5 P(x = y) with x drawn from the non-responder arm, so 0.5 means no separation at all.

## What this sample size rules out

A p-value above the threshold says a difference was not detected.
It does not say there is none, and on its own it is not a result anyone can act on.
The intervals are what bound the claim.

Each interval in the results table is marginal: it covers its own population at 95% and says nothing about the others.
The widest of them reaches 1.10 percentage points, in `monocyte`.
Reading all 5 of them at once is a different claim, though, and one they do not support: 5 marginal 95% intervals guarantee only 75% joint coverage.
Recomputing each at alpha / 5, which is 99% apiece, restores 95% for the set, and the widest then reaches 1.32 percentage points, in `monocyte`.
At 95% simultaneous confidence, then, the data exclude any true shift larger than 1.32 percentage points in every population at once; the marginal bounds in the table are tighter because each covers only one population.
For scale, `monocyte` sits at roughly 19.95% of the cells in a sample, so that bound is about 6.6% of the population's own frequency.
A cohort this size can therefore exclude a large relative difference and not a small one.

## Secondary view: each timepoint on its own

Benjamini-Hochberg is applied within each timepoint, not across them, so a q-value below is corrected over the same family size as the headline and not over three times it.
The timepoints are still not independent of one another, since the same subjects appear in all of them, so this is a consistency check rather than three separate studies.

That is not a contradiction: the interval covers one population at alpha, the q-value is adjusted across every population tested, and the q-value is what governs the conclusion.

## Caveats worth stating

The frequencies sum to 100 by construction, so they are not independent measurements: a shift in one implies compensating shifts in the others.
Closure constrains the covariance structure, since each part's covariances with the rest must sum to minus its own variance, so the parts cannot all be positively related to one another.
That does not make every pair negatively correlated, but it does mean the positive regression dependence Benjamini-Hochberg is proved under is not something this design establishes.
So the same p-values were re-corrected under Benjamini-Yekutieli, which holds under arbitrary dependence, as a check on that assumption rather than as a replacement for the headline correction.
In the headline family the smallest q-value moves from 0.885 to 1.000.
Across the per-timepoint families the smallest becomes 0.165, at t = 14.
No q-value reaches alpha = 0.05 under either correction, so the conclusion above does not rest on the dependence assumption.

Mann-Whitney tests stochastic dominance, and is a test of medians only when the two distributions have the same shape.
The medians are reported anyway because they are what a reader wants, and the shift, which is what the test estimates, is reported beside them.

This says nothing about absolute counts, about populations outside this panel of five, or about a subgroup the cohort does not isolate.
It is a statement about relative frequencies in the samples that were measured.
